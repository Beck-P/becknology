What it takes

What's realistic, and in what order

All of this can be built. Some of it is engine work, done once and every world gets it. The rest is art, done for each thing. Here is how the pieces break down.

PieceWhat it takesSize
Rebuilt placesEach place's scene script remade the way these were: shaped terrain instead of flat ground, richer kit pieces (houses, walls, trees, ferns, rocks, banks), more small things. This is the main art job, world by world.Large
Live lightEach scene is rendered once with a few extra passes: six suns through the day, the sky, the lamps, and flat colour. The game mixes them for the hour, the season and the weather instead of showing one baked picture.Medium
FinishThe version you pick: tile size, smooth or toy light, outlines only where one thing stands in front of another, no dot-grid dither. It's a setting on the same passes.Small
WaterA mirror pass from Blender, and the engine adds depth colour, the lap line, drifting light, glints, rings, ice, and light streaking across the water after dark.Medium
Weather and seasonsSnow settling on whatever faces up, grass and leaves turning, rain and fog, clouds drifting over the sunlit parts.Small
GlowSoft light and HD-2D: bloom on lit windows and lamps, depth of field, vignette. Screen effects over the finished frame.Small
PeopleThe round chibis: the same Blender models with rounder heads and faces painted after the render. Every sheet, the pilot's included, re-renders with them.Medium
BuildingsThe richer house kit, then each village re-rendered.Medium
Postcard viewsA low side camera on the same scene, the sky painted behind, and the camera move as a short cutscene.Medium

The order I'd do it in

  1. Pick a version: tile size, and smooth or toy light.
  2. Live light, finish, glow and water in the engine, with Millpond Farm as the first scene.
  3. Rebuild the places world by world with the richer kit, starting with Verdance.
  4. The round people.
  5. The richer building kit, world by world.
  6. Postcard views and their camera moves, starting with fishing.

How close this is to the game

Close. These are made with the game's own Blender kit and models, and rendered the same way. The new parts are the richer kit pieces, the extra light passes, and the code that lights them, which would move into the game's renderer.

The frames here are one screen each; a game world is many screens, so rebuilding a whole world is bigger than one of these.
