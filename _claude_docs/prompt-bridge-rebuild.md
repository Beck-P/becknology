# Prompt: rebuild a Bridge area in the new look

Use: start a session with "Follow _claude_docs/prompt-bridge-rebuild.md for the area <name>" (an area below, or a new one in the same form).

You're one of several sessions bringing Beck's Bridge game (this repo: github.com/Beck-P/becknology, the game is `apps/bridge`) into its new look, area by area. Beck's ask: "kick off all of the revamps and rebuilds for the current areas to match [Look Book 2]… just exteriors should need improvement for now."

THE TARGET. Beck picked Look Book 2's "toy" version with soft light: our own little 3D model world (the game's Blender kit, 32 px a tile, the 45° camera, the chibi people), finished like toys, in soft light — and places that are far richer than today's: land with shape (terraces, banks, ponds sunk into the ground with reedy pebbly edges — never a circle in a field), vegetation in layers, buildings from the richer house kit, small life everywhere. "Swing larger… still in our 3D style… less boxy and thrown together."

READ FIRST, in this order:
1. `.claude/CLAUDE.md` (repo rules: ship every change to main, tests), `apps/bridge/CLAUDE.md` — especially *North star*, *Pre-rendered Blender scenes* (all its bullets, incl. the detail standard), *Live light*, *Making a new place* (incl. *Re-render or rebuild?*), and the sections about your area's worlds.
2. `tools/bridge-art/CLAUDE.md` (the house style in brief) and `tools/bridge-art/dioramas/README.md`.
3. LOOK at the reference images with your Read tool: `tools/bridge-art/dioramas/ref/*.png` — the Look Book places in the toy finish at noon / golden hour / night. The ones closest to your area are listed below. Then read the diorama scripts that built them (`tools/bridge-art/dioramas/*.py`) and the house kit `tools/bridge-art/assets_house.py` — that's how the richness is made.
4. Your area's current scene scripts (`tools/bridge-art/scenes/…`), their kits (`assets_*.py`) and their current renders (`apps/bridge/assets/scenes/<id>/*.webp` — look at them).

SETUP. Blender's Python module: if `/home/user/BVENV/bin/python` doesn't exist, `python3.13 -m venv /home/user/BVENV && /home/user/BVENV/bin/pip install bpy==5.2.2` (numpy comes with it). `scene_post.py` runs on the system `python3` (numpy + Pillow; `pip install numpy pillow` if missing). Chromium for the tests is preinstalled (see tools/tests/README.md). The machine has 4 cores: run ONE Blender render at a time, in the background (`nice -n 5 … &` with a log, or run_in_background), and poll its log; a big outdoor scene takes 30-90 min at full quality — iterate at low samples first (`light_samples` in the script and `SCENE_LIT_SAMPLES=8`, or crop your look with a small test script like the dioramas).

FOR EACH PLACE (a rebuild unless it says re-render):
1. Rebuild its scene script to the reference standard, KEEPING THE MAP: the tile layout and everything you can do there — every interaction, door, spawn, NPC actor, quest/business/people label (`{world, label}` matches in assets/quests.json, people.json, businesses.json), walker routes, farm plots, fishing spots, piers. The rebuild is the art, not the map. Richer houses at the same footprints and doors (`HK.rich_house` takes `assets_town.house`'s arguments; `HK.install()` swaps it in for a whole script), shaped land and banks, real ponds/streams named `pond_water`/`stream_water` (so the live light gives them Look Book water), layered vegetation inside `k.foliage(...)`, clutter and small life. Name parts so the toy finish reads them (`floor_*`, `path`, `grass`, `cobble`, `roof_*`…).
2. Iterate on the look with fast low-sample renders; look at crops at 2× next to the reference images; keep going until it would sit beside them and belong. Don't stop at "a bit better".
3. Final render with live light (scenes with a day already pass `cycle=`; keep it), then `python3 tools/bridge-art/scene_post.py RENDER_DIR apps/bridge/assets/scenes/<id>`. The script also rewrites its world JSON (`apps/bridge/assets/worlds/<id>.json`) — check that diff is what you meant.
4. Verify: `python3 tools/bridge-art/audit_worlds.py` (nothing newly unreachable), `node tools/tests/run.js world` (all pass; for Lumar islands also make sure `world/sea.js` and `world/arrive.js` pass), and screenshots in the game at dawn, noon, golden hour and night and in each season (`BridgeLit._force({ hour: 7|12.4|17.9|1, season: 'spring'|'summer'|'autumn'|'winter' })` — see `tools/tests/world/litlight.js` for how; it takes a world id). Look at them. Check the live compose isn't too slow (`BridgeLit._stats(id).ms`).
5. Ship that place before starting the next: commit on your branch, `git fetch origin main && git merge origin/main`, re-run the quick checks if main brought changes, then push your branch and fast-forward main (`git push origin HEAD:main`; if it's rejected because main moved, fetch/merge/push again). Push to main once per finished place, not per tweak (Vercel's daily build limit). In `_claude_docs/requests.md`, in the *the new look* section's backlog rows, move your place from "To do" to "Done" — touch nothing else there.

SHARED CODE. Other sessions are rebuilding other areas at the same time (the areas at the end of this file). Don't touch their scenes or worlds. Put new builders in your scene script or a new kit file of your own (e.g. `tools/bridge-art/assets_<area>_rich.py`); if you improve a shared kit (`assets_town.py`, `assets_isle.py`, `assets_coast.py`, `assets_house.py`, `scenekit.py`, `scene_post.py`…), keep the change additive and backward compatible (every other place still renders the same with it), and merge main often. Don't change the engine (`apps/bridge/js/litscene.js`, `scene.js`) unless something is truly broken — then keep it minimal and say so in your commit.

WHEN YOU'RE DONE with your area (or if something blocks you), finish with a short report: each place, what changed, before/after screenshot paths, and anything left. Interiors are out of scope.
